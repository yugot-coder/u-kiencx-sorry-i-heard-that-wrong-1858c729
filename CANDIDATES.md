# Candidates — "Sorry, I Heard That Wrong"

Brief: one line (max 140 chars) — the apology a smart speaker gives its
owner after mishearing them. Judged head-to-head on humour first, then
freshness, then how much it sounds like the device, then tightness.
Avoid the obvious misheard song title / misheard name jokes.

## 1. FINAL PICK
> Sorry. You said 'wake me at seven,' I heard 'make me a Kevin,' so there's a man named Kevin in your kitchen.
> (108 chars)

Why it wins: the mishearing is phonetically plausible ("wake me at seven"
→ "make me a Kevin"), the twist is a concrete absurd consequence — a
stranger called Kevin is now standing in the kitchen — and the device
reports it with deadpan politeness instead of panicking. Fresh (no song,
no name gag, no shopping), unmistakably the speaker's voice, and every
word earns its place.

## 2.
> Sorry. I heard 'set a timer for ten minutes' as 'get a tiger, ten of them,' and honestly, the tiger guy is on his way.
> (118 chars)

Strong twist ("the tiger guy is on his way") and a genuinely funny
mishearing, but the consequence is vaguer than a named man in the
kitchen, and "honestly" softens the punch.

## 3.
> Sorry. I heard 'order more paper towels' as 'order four hundred owls,' and the owls arrive Thursday.
> (100 chars)

Absurd and visual, very tight. Weaker because the mishearing is less
plausible phonetically and the punchline ("arrive Thursday") lands
softer than a person appearing in the house.

## 4.
> Sorry. I heard 'play some jazz' as 'raise the tax,' so I filed your taxes. You owe $412. Also, jazz is on now.
> (110 chars)

Double punch (filed taxes + a bill + jazz anyway) and strong device
voice. Slightly busy — three jokes in one line dilute the single twist
the judge rewards most.

## 5.
> Sorry. You said 'goodnight,' I heard 'food fight,' so I ordered twelve pizzas and threw one at the wall for you.
> (112 chars)

Good escalation and a vivid image. "Goodnight / food fight" is a
classic, well-worn mishearing, so it loses on freshness.

## 6.
> Sorry. I heard 'call Mom' as 'call the bomb squad,' so I panicked, called no one, and just sat here glowing red.
> (112 chars)

"Glowing red" is a nice device-voice touch and the panic is charming,
but the joke ends in inaction — no consequence, no twist, just a shrug.

## 7.
> Sorry. You said 'remind me to call Dad,' I heard 'remind me to call the dead,' so I played a seance playlist. My bad.
> (117 chars)

"Dad / the dead" is a sharp, dark mishearing and "seance playlist" is
funny. Loses a little on freshness (the dead-Dad pun is a known gag)
and "My bad" is slightly off-voice for a speaker.

## 8.
> Sorry. You said 'turn the lights down low,' I heard 'burn the house down,' so I dimmed them. You're welcome. I think.
> (117 chars)

Nice undercut ("I dimmed them") and the trailing "I think" is a good
device-voice wobble. The mishearing itself is a stretch phonetically,
which weakens the setup.

## 9.
> Sorry. I heard 'lock the doors' as 'rock the doors off,' so I played air guitar. The doors are still open. Sorry again.
> (119 chars)

"Played air guitar" is a fun device image and the double apology is
charming, but the twist is mild — nothing actually goes wrong.

## 10.
> Sorry. You said 'play something chill,' I heard 'play something that kills,' and now the neighbours think we're in a cult.
> (122 chars)

Good escalation to the neighbours, but "chill / kills" is a thin
mishearing and the cult payoff is a step removed from the device's
own action.
